Units and techniques

You draft 3 of these 5 units. They grow during the run by learning techniques: every won fight (except the boss and the Moon Shrine) offers 3 that your units can learn; one unit learns one, or you skip for 8 gold. Shops sell them too (15 gold common, 24 rare). Each technique belongs to one line, and some need another first. A unit's level is the number of techniques it has learned; forgetting one (at a shop) keeps the level.

Enemy AI

How enemies decide what to do. The rules every enemy shares come first; each behaviour below only lists what it does differently.

The enemy turn

  • At the start of the enemy phase, burning ground and poison tick, then every enemy gets its 1 AP back (unused AP doesn't carry over) and its moves (1, or 2 with Swift; none if slowed).
  • Next, every bomb on the board explodes, one by one (a blast sets off other bombs and mines inside it).
  • Then the enemies act one at a time, in the order they entered the battle. Each finishes its whole turn before the next starts. Units raised or split off during this phase wait until the next enemy turn.
  • A turn is a loop: the enemy picks a target, does the first thing on its behaviour's list that it can, and starts over. It stops when it runs out of AP and moves, or nothing on the list helps. The target is picked again before every action, so after a kill it turns to the next one.
  • Enemies move by your rules: 1 move per tile (moves are separate from AP), no diagonals, stepping out of a tile next to one of your units uses up their remaining moves (zone of control), no moving after spending AP, and pinned or slowed units can't move.
  • Enemies can't see mines, except a goblin standing next to one, which can fling it. One that steps on a mine stops there and sets it off.

Targeting

  1. A unit with Taunt within 2 tiles: the nearest such unit.
  2. Blood Scent (wolves): the lowest-HP unit it can reach and attack this turn, walking up first if needed.
  3. Otherwise the nearest of your units, counted in steps and ignoring rocks. Ties go to the one with the lowest HP, then to the one listed first in your army. If that unit has a shield, it goes for the nearest unshielded unit it can reach this turn instead, and hits the shield only when there is none.

Every behaviour uses this target. Only the Bomber may bomb a different unit when its target can't be bombed, and the Necromancer raises bone piles before it looks for a target at all.

Walking up

  1. If it has its AP and can reach a tile from which it can hit the target, it walks to the cheapest such tile. Ranged units need range and line of sight from that tile.
  2. Otherwise it walks as close to the target as it can: to the reachable tile with the shortest walk to any tile next to the target, the cheapest of those. That walk goes around rocks but through units, so an enemy stuck behind its friends still edges forward.
  3. If no reachable tile is closer than where it stands, it stays put.

Ranged enemies that walk up (the Crossbowman) walk to the cheapest tile with a shot. They don't look for cover; the Camp layout just starts them behind rocks.

Backing off

Used by enemies that want distance, once per turn, when their target is too close: it moves to the reachable tile furthest from the nearest of your units, the cheapest of those. If no tile is further away than where it stands, it stays.

Getting into range from a distance

Used by enemies that fight from afar, once per turn. They only consider tiles at least their comfort distance from every one of your units:

  1. The cheapest tile from which it can use its ranged action on the target, if it still has the AP for it.
  2. If there is none, the tile closest to the target, if it is closer than where it stands.

Encounters

Battles are on a 6x6 board; you place your army in the back two rows. Kill every enemy to win. Battles bring factions you haven't beaten yet, then normal-strength rematches; Act 1 ends with one of the bosses. Every encounter has a layout template: fixed rules, with the exact rocks and start tiles varied each time.

If the shrine breaks, the fight is lost (no gold) and you take a random curse, but the run goes on. Losing the whole army still ends the run. Enemies go for the shrine unless one of your units is next to them (Taunt still wins). The shrine counts as your ally for Shield Wall and Guard; your own blasts and volleys hurt it. Reinforcements arrive on edge tiles at least 3 steps from it.

Break the Ritual: 3 totems (6 HP) stand on the far half, guarded by goblins. Smash them all before the summoning completes at the start of enemy turn 4: you win, the guards flee, and you get the usual technique pick. If the summoning completes, the fight is lost (no gold) and you take a random curse; the run goes on. Guards go for whichever of your units is within 2 tiles of a totem; their own bombs don't hurt the totems. The shrine and totems can't attack, so they have no zone of control.

Run & map

  • Pick 1 of 3 random banners, then draft 3 different base units (2 with Third Wheel). Army size up to 4.
  • Act 1 has 12 steps; each step offers 2 nodes. At steps 1, 4, 6, 8, 10 both choices are fights, so every run has at least 5 fights before the boss. Step 9 offers one more optional battle; the last step is a boss (Necromancer or Giant).
  • A Battle brings a faction you haven't beaten yet; once you've met them all it's a normal-strength rematch. An Elite (steps 6 and 10) is a harder rematch of a faction you've beaten. Both show their faction when you reach their step.
  • The two scenario fights sit at steps 4 and 8, next to a battle: the Moon Shrine at one, Break the Ritual at the other. Shops and ? events fill the rest.
  • Won battle: 10 gold + 5 per surviving unit (+5 for an ambush). Gold carried off by escaped cutpurses is lost.
  • Technique pick: every won fight except the boss and the Moon Shrine offers 3 techniques your units can learn; one unit learns one and goes up a level, or skip for 8 gold.
  • Shop: 3 techniques (15 gold common, 24 rare), 3 items, forgetting a technique for 8 gold, a random base-unit recruit for 15 gold (if the army has room), and one banner you don't hold yet for 20 gold.
  • Every battle starts at full HP and dead units come back. Techniques, levels, items and shrine HP last the whole run.

Banners

A banner bends a rule for the whole army for the rest of the run. You start with one (pick 1 of 3), shops sell one you don't hold for 20 gold, and an elite offers one instead of its technique. No limit on how many you hold; each is held once. Taking Third Wheel with more units than it allows: choose who stays, the rest leave.

Battlefields

Every battle, elite and boss (and an ambush) has one battlefield rule, named on its map node from the start of the run, so you can route towards or around it. The scenario fights have none. A step's fights never share a battlefield or repeat one from the step before. Every rule works the same for both sides.

Enemy AI: on Edge of the World an enemy that can push one of your units off the board with Bull Rush or Upheaval does that first. In a Storm, enemies never stop on a marked tile and step off one before acting.

Battle rules

  • Moves are separate from AP: every unit walks 1 tile per turn (2 with Swift, +1 with Spring Boots), 4 directions, no diagonals. Units can walk through their allies (not through objectives) but can't stop on them, and never through enemies. Move first: once a unit spends AP (an attack or action) it can't move again that turn, for both sides. Unused moves don't carry over. AP pays for attacks and actions: every unit has 1 AP, so one attack or action per turn, and unused AP doesn't carry over. Blink and Vanish are free, usable only before the unit has acted.
  • Cooldowns: basic attacks have none. Every other action, yours and the enemies', is ready again a set number of the unit's own turns after it's used (cooldown 2: used now, ready again two turns later).
  • Side phases: all your units act (one at a time), then all enemies.
  • Zone of control: stepping out of a tile next to an enemy uses up all your remaining moves, for both sides. You can always back off one tile, but you can't slip past.
  • Ranged attacks need line of sight (rocks block); Arcane Bolt ignores it. Bombs, boulders and fireballs are lobbed.
  • Mines: 4 damage (5 with Big Mines) to whoever stands on its tile, set off when an enemy steps on one (or a blast, fling or fireball sets it off). Bombs explode at the start of the next enemy turn and hit only their own tile. Fireballs and Volley hit a plus: the tile and its 4 neighbours. Blasts set each other off.
  • Push: a unit pushed into a rock, unit or the edge stops there and takes 2 damage. An enemy pushed onto a mine sets it off. Weight: a push moves a medium unit 1 tile, a small one 2 and a huge one none (no collision damage either); Battering Ram adds 1 tile to your pushes.
  • Poison: 2 damage at the start of the target's turn, 2 times. Burning ground: 1 damage at the start of the turn of whoever stands on it.
  • Pinned units (Pinning Shot) and slowed units (Chill) can't move on their next turn.
  • Vanished units (rogues) can't be targeted or attacked by enemies, though area blasts still hit them. Attacking reveals them.
  • Hover any enemy to see what it plans to do next.
